CHAPTER 01 / 05
01 / THE ORIGIN

The problem wasn’t income. It was visibility.

Six years in real estate taught me to plan around a reality most budgeting tools assume away: the timing and size of a paycheck can change every month.

Some months bring multiple commissions. Others bring none. Bills, taxes, and business expenses keep moving either way. I was piecing together different numbers just to answer one question: What can I actually afford to do next?

DESIGN OPPORTUNITY / 01

Give independent earners a clearer view of cash, upcoming commitments, and irregular income—without asking them to think like someone on a fixed salary.

ORIGINAL BRAND IDENTITY / HDSN
CHAPTER 02 / 05
02 / THE PRODUCT

A financial picture. Not another spreadsheet.

HDSN is a financial dashboard in development for entrepreneurs whose income doesn't arrive on a predictable schedule.

My north star: make the essentials understandable at a glance. What's available? What needs to be set aside? Which expenses and write-offs need to be recorded? What's expected—and when? The first release is being shaped around those questions, not a long list of features.

PRODUCT SCOPE / FIRST RELEASE

A connected workflow for commission income, check allocations, tax set-asides, expenses and write-offs, mileage, and upcoming bills. Manual entry keeps the first version independent of bank integrations; the experience is still being refined ahead of beta.

CHAPTER 04 / 05
04 / THE BUILD

The prototype challenged the plan.

Building HDSN made the design questions more specific. A flow that looks straightforward on paper can behave very differently once data and logic are involved.

I shaped the early experience and used AI-assisted tools to prototype and implement it. I also worked through the supporting product stack, tested flows, and investigated issues as they appeared. My role spans product direction, UX decisions, testing, and iteration—not a claim that I hand-coded the entire application.

A CONCRETE CHALLENGE

Early versions surfaced discrepancies in commission calculations and onboarding-state persistence. I had to move beyond whether a screen looked right: define the expected result, trace where behavior diverged, and check changes against real scenarios.

A polished interface means very little if the numbers behind it can't be trusted.

ISSUES IDENTIFIED DURING PROTOTYPING / FINAL RESOLUTION AND SECURITY VERIFICATION NOT CLAIMED

05 / OUT IN THE WORLD
GOOD COMPANY CREATIVE MARKET / OCT 2026

An early signal. Not the final answer.

I introduced HDSN's concept at Good Company and invited entrepreneurs to think through how they allocate income. I wanted reactions from the people I was building for—before asking anyone to use the product.

30+WAITLIST SIGNUPS
WHAT THE CONVERSATIONS REVEALED

People responded to the challenge of allocating irregular income and keeping a clear financial picture. The conversations helped me sharpen the problem and hear where the concept resonated.

WHAT ISN'T VALIDATED YET

Waitlist interest is not evidence of usability, retention, or willingness to pay. The next step is a refined product and structured beta testing to learn what actually holds up in daily use.
